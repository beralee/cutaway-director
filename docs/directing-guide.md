# Direct a shot the viewer can explain

Start with the sentence you want someone to say after watching. For the [seal demo](../examples/seal-proof.mp4), that sentence is “the ring sits between the lid and the base.” That tells you which parts to build, where the camera belongs and when to stop moving.

| Time in the demo | What the viewer sees | Directing choice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–2 seconds | A closed assembly begins to open. | Keep the base visible as the reference object. |
| 2–4 seconds | The lid lifts and the orange ring separates from the base. | Give the ring its own color and silhouette. |
| 4–7 seconds | The camera settles. A leader points to the ring. | Place the label after the viewer can see its target. Hold it long enough to read. |
| 7–10 seconds | The ring returns to its seat and the lid closes. | Replay the relationship in reverse so the viewer can locate the hidden part again. |

## Use the same approach for a different subject

**Structure.** “Explain how a ball bearing lets one ring turn inside another. Keep the housing oriented the same way. Reveal one ball, mark its contact surfaces, then restore the assembly.”

**Comparison.** “Make an English explainer about two equal-size spheres with different masses. Use the supplied assumptions and units. Stop on one comparable frame, label the forces, then repeat the experiment without air.”

**Process.** “Explain an invented ticket system. Follow request A17 to a ledger with three rows. Hold as it meets the matching row. Send the result back along the same path. Label the scene as a simplified model.”

**Place.** “Start with a wide view of a restored mill, travel to one wheel tooth, then freeze and show how it engages the next gear. Use the attached references. Mark reconstructed features and keep visible landmarks consistent.”

## Give feedback that is easy to execute

“The shot feels rushed” leaves several possible edits. “From 3 to 5 seconds, ease the camera to a stop before the leader appears. Keep the geometry, voice recording and final hold” identifies the controls and the assets worth preserving.
